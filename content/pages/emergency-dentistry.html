Emergency Dentistry in San Francisco

A dental emergency is any injury or infection in your mouth that needs treatment right away to stop pain, save a tooth, or keep an infection from spreading. This page explains how to triage your symptoms, what to do in the first 60 minutes, when to call our 24/7 hotline, and when a hospital emergency room is the safer choice.

Key takeaways

  • A knocked-out adult tooth should be replanted in its socket within about 15 minutes if you can do it safely, or stored in milk — never tap water — until you reach care.
  • Severe tooth pain, a broken tooth, or a localized abscess is usually treated fastest by a dentist, because an emergency room can only relieve pain and infection, not repair the tooth.
  • Go to a hospital emergency room for swelling that affects your breathing or swallowing, a high fever with facial swelling, uncontrolled bleeding, a suspected jaw fracture, or any head injury.
  • Our 24/7 hotline, (800) 586-4621, reaches a clinician who can give first-aid instructions at once and arrange same-day relief.
  • Most dental emergencies are preventable: 1 in 5 US adults aged 20 to 64 has at least one untreated cavity, and tooth disorders drive roughly 1.94 million emergency department visits a year.[1][2]

First 60 minutes

What to do in the first hour of a dental emergency

The minutes after an injury decide whether a tooth survives. These are the steps our clinical team gives over the phone, in the order that matters most.

emergency

1. Stop the bleeding and control pain

Bite firmly on clean gauze or a cloth for 10 to 15 minutes. Apply a cold compress to the outside of your face for 20 minutes at a time. Take an over-the-counter pain reliever if you have no allergies to it.

dentistry

2. Handle a knocked-out tooth correctly

Pick it up by the crown, never the root. Rinse gently with milk or saline — not soap, and do not scrub. Replant it root-first into the socket and bite on gauze, or drop it into a container of milk.[3]

schedule

3. Understand the clock

The cells that let a tooth reattach, the periodontal ligament, begin to die after about 30 minutes out of the mouth and are likely non-viable after 60. That is why immediate replantation is best.[4]

call

4. Call before you travel

Phoning ahead lets us prepare the chair, the imaging, and any needed medication, so your appointment is faster and calmer. If you are unsure whether it is urgent, our team will tell you honestly.

ER vs dentist

When to go to the ER and when to see a dentist

A hospital emergency room treats the medical emergency; a dentist fixes the tooth. Knowing which one you need saves both money and time.

Choose the emergency room when

  • Swelling spreads to your eye, neck, or throat, or makes breathing or swallowing hard.
  • You have a high fever, chills, or feel systemically unwell with facial swelling.
  • Bleeding will not stop after sustained pressure.
  • You suspect a broken jaw, or you lost consciousness or hit your head.

Choose a dentist when

  • Pain is coming from a specific tooth but your airway and swallowing are normal.
  • A tooth is chipped, cracked, loose, or knocked out.
  • You have a dental abscess without facial swelling or fever.
  • You lost a filling or crown and the tooth is sensitive but not dangerous.

Emergency rooms are designed for medical stabilization, not definitive dental repair, and the cost reflects that: dental emergency department visits in the US cost an estimated $3.4 billion in 2019 alone, largely for conditions routine care could have prevented.[5] When the situation is safe, we can resolve the cause — a root canal, a same-day crown, or an extraction — in a single visit with transparent pricing.
