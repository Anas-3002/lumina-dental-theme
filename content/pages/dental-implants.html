Dental Implants in San Francisco

A dental implant is a small titanium post that replaces a missing tooth root and fuses with your jawbone so a crown can be attached without cutting down neighboring teeth. Key takeaways

  • An implant replaces the tooth root, not just the visible tooth, so it preserves jawbone and leaves healthy neighbors untouched.
  • Roughly 178 million Americans are missing at least one tooth, and about 40 million are missing all of theirs.[1]
  • Modern titanium implants show a pooled survival rate near 95% at 10 years and about 92% beyond 20 years.[2][3]
  • All-on-4 places a full arch of fixed teeth on four implants, often without grafting, with 92.5% implant survival reported at 12 years.[4]
  • The two rules that decide long-term success are controlled health conditions and daily hygiene, because the gum tissue around an implant — not the implant itself — is what fails.
The basics

What exactly is a dental implant?

A dental implant is a biocompatible titanium post placed surgically into the jawbone to act as an artificial tooth root. It is not the tooth you see: the implant is the anchor, an abutment is the connector, and a custom crown is the visible tooth on top.

dentistry

The implant post

A titanium screw, usually 3.5–5 mm wide, surgically set into the jaw. Titanium is used because bone bonds to it directly without rejecting it.[5]

settings

The abutment

A precision connector that screws into the healed implant and holds the final restoration. It is fitted only after the post has fully integrated.

The crown

A custom ceramic or zirconia tooth shaded to match your smile. At Lumina we mill many of these in-house — see our same-day crowns service.

Process & candidacy

The implant process and who it suits

Implant treatment is planned in three phases, but the timeline depends on your bone and your health — not a fixed schedule.

Phase 1–3: placement to final tooth

  1. Surgical placement. We image the site in 3D, then place the post under local anesthesia. Most patients need only mild or sedation dentistry and return to normal routines the next day.
  2. Osseointegration. The bone grows around the post for about three to six months. A temporary tooth keeps the gap closed.
  3. Restoration. We attach the abutment and the final crown or bridge once the implant is stable.

You are likely a good candidate if…

  • You have one or more missing teeth and enough bone to anchor a post.
  • Your general health is stable. Your health matters more than your age, but uncontrolled diabetes, active gum disease, bleeding disorders, some bone diseases and certain medications can affect healing.[6]
  • You do not currently smoke, since tobacco slows healing and lowers long-term success.[5]
  • You can commit to daily cleaning and yearly maintenance visits.

If your jaw has thinned, a graft or a graftless design such as All-on-4 may still make treatment possible.

Biology & longevity

Osseointegration, and how long implants last

Osseointegration is the process in which living bone grows directly against the implant surface and locks it in place — the word means “combines with the bone.”[6]

A 10-year study of 511 titanium implants with a modern roughened surface reported 98.8% survival and 97.0% success.[3] Across 23 long-term studies, the pooled mean survival was 94.6% over follow-up of up to 20 years, with about 70% of losses occurring after the implant had already been loaded.[2] The message is simple: the implant itself rarely fails early. What threatens it later is peri-implantitis — inflammation and bone loss in the gum tissue around the implant — which is why hygiene and recall visits matter.

Compare options

Single implant, implant bridge, or All-on-4?

All-on-4 is a full-arch technique that supports an entire jaw of teeth on four implants — two upright in front, two tilted at the back to use denser bone and often avoid grafting.[4] Use the table to see where each option fits.

Option Implants used Best for Reported survival
Single implant + crown 1 One missing tooth, healthy neighbors 95–99% at 10 years[3]
Implant bridge 2+ Several adjacent missing teeth Above 90% at 10 years[2]
All-on-4 full arch 4 per jaw Most or all teeth missing, or failing 92.5% at 12 years[4]

In a separate meta-analysis, full-arch survival was 98.14% for All-on-4 and 97.50% for All-on-6, and mean bone loss around All-on-4 implants was about 1.19 mm at 10 years.[7][4] For patients with extensive tooth loss, comparing a fixed full-arch result with a removable denture is a genuine conversation, and we plan it with you rather than for you.
