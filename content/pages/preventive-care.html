Preventive & Hygiene Care

Preventive care is the routine work that stops problems before they start: examinations, low-dose digital X-rays and 3D imaging, professional cleanings, fluoride, sealants, and oral cancer screening. This page explains what happens at each visit, which imaging you actually need, and the home habits that keep small issues from becoming expensive ones.

Key takeaways

  • Cavities are among the most common diseases people live with: 1 in 5 US adults aged 20 to 64 has at least one untreated cavity, and nearly all adults over 65 have had one.[1]
  • Dental sealants prevent 80% of cavities on the back teeth for two years, and still prevent 50% for up to four years.[2]
  • Gum disease is common and often painless: 42.2% of US adults aged 30 and older have some form of periodontitis.[3]
  • X-rays are prescribed for a reason, not on a fixed schedule. Your history and clinical exam decide which images you need and when.[4]
  • Oral cancer screening is part of every comprehensive exam, because about 60% of oral and pharyngeal cancers are already advanced when found.[5]
The visit

What a preventive exam and cleaning includes

A hygiene visit is a diagnostic appointment and a maintenance appointment at once. We assess what is happening, then prevent it from getting worse.

health_and_safety

Examination

We chart every tooth, check restorations, and measure periodontal probing depth — the gap between gum and tooth, in millimeters — to detect gum disease before it hurts.[3]

dentistry

Cleaning (prophylaxis)

Prophylaxis means the professional removal of plaque and hardened tartar from above and just below the gumline, followed by polishing. If pockets are deep, we plan a therapeutic cleaning instead.

Imaging

Digital X-rays and 3D CBCT imaging

Cone beam CT (CBCT) is a three-dimensional dental X-ray that shows bone, roots, and nerves in cross-section. It carries a higher dose than a two-dimensional image, so we use it only when it will change your treatment.

Imaging type Typical effective dose What it shows When we use it
Periapical (intraoral) About 0.6–0.7 µSv[6] One or two teeth, root and bone detail Targeted diagnosis and treatment checks
Panoramic About 4.1 µSv[6] The whole jaw in one 2D image Wisdom teeth, orthodontic and surgical planning
3D CBCT About 88–170 µSv, depending on device[6] Bone volume, roots, nerves and sinuses in 3D Implant placement and complex cases only

We follow the ALARA and ALADA principles — as low as reasonably achievable, and as low as diagnostically acceptable — which means the smallest dose that still answers the clinical question. An ADA expert panel updated its guidance in 2024: clinicians should justify every image on a clear clinical benefit, and shielding like thyroid collars is no longer recommended because it can block the beam and lead to extra exposures.[7] The same principle governs frequency.

“Dentists should not prescribe routine dental radiographs at preset intervals for all patients.”

— The Use of Dental Radiographs, JADA / ADA–FDA guidelines
Fluoride & sealants

Fluoride and sealants: the strongest preventive tools

Fluoride varnish strengthens enamel and reduces cavities in primary teeth by about one-third, and the US Preventive Services Task Force gives fluoride varnish a Grade B recommendation for children from the eruption of the first tooth.[1][8]

Sealants are thin coatings painted into the grooves of back teeth, where 9 in 10 cavities occur. They prevent 80% of cavities for two years and continue protecting against 50% for up to four years; children without sealants have almost three times as many cavities in their first molars.[2] They are most effective when placed soon after the adult molars erupt, around age 6 and again around age 12.

Prevention goes beyond fillings. Straightening crowded teeth makes them easier to clean — compare options on our Invisalign clear aligners page — and professional whitening is safest after a clean, healthy exam, which you can read about under laser teeth whitening.

Oral cancer screening

Oral cancer screening at every exam

We examine your lips, tongue, gums, cheeks, and throat for sores or patches that are not healing. It takes a minute and it matters: about 60% of oral and pharyngeal cancers are already moderately advanced or metastatic when diagnosed, and the overall 5-year relative survival rate is 69%.[5]

Found while still localized, survival is far higher — 95% for localized lip cancer and 88% for localized tongue cancer.[9] Most oropharyngeal squamous cell cancers in the US are now linked to HPV, so screening matters even if you have never used tobacco. Tell us about any lesion that has lasted more than two weeks.

Home care

Home care between visits

  • Brush twice daily with fluoride toothpaste, for two minutes, reaching the gumline.
  • Clean between your teeth daily with floss or interdental brushes — 53.1% of adults who do not floss regularly have periodontitis, versus fewer with the habit.[3]
  • Limit sugary and acidic snacks between meals, especially drinks sipped all day.
  • Do not ignore bleeding gums; they are a sign of inflammation, not normal brushing.
  • Keep professional cleanings on schedule — see new patient information for how to start.
