First Visit

What happens at your first appointment

A first visit is a thorough baseline, not a quick glance. Following the American Dental Association's structure for a checkup, we review your medical history, examine your teeth, gums and mouth, and decide whether imaging is needed. Your dentist then screens for oral cancer across your tongue, jaw and neck.[1] One in five adults aged 20 to 64 has at least one untreated cavity.[3]

Key takeaways

  • Your first visit combines a comprehensive exam, digital X-rays, a 3D scan and a written plan.
  • A full first appointment usually runs about 60 to 75 minutes.
  • Bring photo ID, your insurance card, a medication list and any recent X-rays.
  • The $99 New Patient Gateway covers the exam, imaging and consultation.

Step by Step

Your first visit, step by step

Appointment times vary, but a first visit follows this sequence.

Step What happens Typical time
1. Arrival and check-in We confirm your details and settle you into a private suite. 10 minutes
2. Medical and dental history We review conditions, medications, allergies and past treatment. 10 minutes
3. Digital X-rays and 3D scan Imaging and an intraoral scan build your diagnostic baseline. 15 minutes
4. Comprehensive exam Tooth-by-tooth assessment with an oral cancer screening. 20 minutes
5. Gum and bone assessment Periodontal charting records gum health around each tooth. 10 minutes
6. Treatment plan and costs We review findings, options and prices, and you decide. 15 minutes

Your Privacy

Medical history and your privacy

We ask about conditions such as diabetes or heart disease, pregnancy, medications, allergies, past surgery and habits like smoking. Many change what is safe and effective, and some show their first signs in the mouth.[1]

Your information is protected by HIPAA. As a covered entity, the Privacy Rule covers your health information whether electronic, written or spoken.[5] You receive our Notice of Privacy Practices at your first appointment, and can request a copy any time.[6]

The Privacy Rule gives you, with few exceptions, the right to inspect, review, and receive a copy of your medical records and billing records that are held by health plans and health care providers covered by the Privacy Rule.

U.S. Department of Health & Human Services, "Your Medical Records"[7]

Insurance verification before you arrive

We verify your dental benefits before your appointment so your estimate is not a guess. Dentists use the pre-authorization process to determine coverage; it is a good-faith estimate, not a guarantee, because benefit levels apply at the time of service.[8]
