Procedures

A comprehensive exam is a full baseline, not a quick glance. Your dentist reviews your medical history, examines your teeth, gums and mouth, and decides whether X-rays are needed. The visit includes an oral cancer screening of your tongue, whole mouth, jaw and neck.[2]
X-rays are taken only when your dentist believes they will provide the diagnostic information needed, following 2024 recommendations from an ADA expert panel. Dental X-rays use very low radiation doses, and digital equipment reduces exposure further. If a previous dentist has recent images, we may request copies instead.[3]
We scan your tooth digitally, design the crown in software, then mill it from a solid porcelain block in our in-house milling unit while you wait. You leave with the crown bonded in a single visit, so there is no temporary crown and no second appointment. Learn more about same-day crowns.

Emergencies

A dental emergency is any problem needing prompt attention: severe or worsening toothache, a knocked-out or cracked tooth, uncontrolled bleeding, swelling in your face or jaw, or a lost filling that leaves a sharp edge. If you are unsure, call us and describe what is happening. See emergency dentistry.[4]
Act quickly and keep the tooth moist. If you can, place it back in the socket without touching the root; otherwise keep it between your cheek and gum, in milk, or in an ADA Seal-accepted preservation product, then get to a dentist right away.[4]
We hold emergency appointments every day and aim to see urgent cases within about two hours during opening hours. Call (800) 586-4621 and describe your symptoms so we can prioritise you, and we will fit you in at the earliest safe time.[4]
