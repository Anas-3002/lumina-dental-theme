Invisalign Clear Aligners

Invisalign is a system of clear, removable plastic trays that straighten teeth gradually without fixed metal braces. This page explains how aligners move teeth, how long treatment takes, how to care for your aligners, and how the system compares with conventional braces.

Key takeaways

  • Aligners work by applying gentle, controlled pressure that remodels the bone around a tooth, moving it a fraction of a millimeter at a time.[1]
  • You wear each aligner about 22 hours a day, changing it roughly every one to two weeks.[1][2]
  • Mild to moderate cases often finish within 6 to 18 months; complex bite corrections take longer.[3]
  • Compliance is the single biggest predictor of results — leave the aligners out and treatment stalls.[4]
  • Invisalign and braces cost roughly the same; most dental plans that cover braces cover aligners the same way.[5]
How it works

How clear aligners move your teeth

Orthodontic tooth movement is a biological process, not a mechanical push. Every appliance — aligner or brace — relies on the same biology.

route

1. A digital plan

We scan your teeth and software builds a step-by-step path from today's positions to the target. One aligner is made for each stage of that path.[6]

health_and_safety

2. Gentle pressure

Each tray nudges the tooth a fraction of a millimeter. The bone in front resorbs and new bone forms behind, so the tooth settles into its new position.

schedule

3. A retainer holds it

Teeth drift back if nothing holds them, so every plan ends with a retainer worn as directed. Retainers are part of the treatment, not an add-on.[7]

“Each aligner is worn for 1 week at a time, at least 22 hours a day, and moves teeth a fraction of a millimeter at a time.”

— American Association of Orthodontists
Timeline

How long does Invisalign take?

Treatment time is set by how far your teeth have to travel, so an honest range beats a single number.

Case complexity Typical aligner time What drives it
Mild crowding or spacing 6–12 months A few teeth, small movements
Moderate alignment 6–18 months[3] Rotation, spacing, some bite work
Complex bite correction Up to 24 months or more[3] Extractions, large movements, root control

For context, fixed braces typically run one to three years.[8] One systematic review reported a mean of 18 months for Invisalign against 24 months for fixed appliances in comparable non-extraction cases, though aligners can take longer on complex extraction cases.[4] Worn properly, aligners are often faster for everyday alignment; for the hardest bites, braces remain more predictable. Either way, a hygiene check-up and cleaning comes first so we scan healthy teeth.

Invisalign versus braces

Aligners are removable; braces are fixed. That single difference shapes comfort, hygiene, and results.

Factor Invisalign clear aligners Fixed braces
Visibility Near-invisible Visible (metal) or discreet (ceramic)
Removable Yes — out to eat, brush, and floss No — fixed to the teeth
Daily wear About 22 hours a day[1] Constant; nothing to remember
Oral hygiene Easy — normal brushing and flossing Harder around brackets and wires
Best for Mild to moderate misalignment Complex bites and large movements
Cost Comparable to braces[5] About $3,000–$7,500[5]

Invisalign suits adults who want discretion and patients with mild to moderate needs; braces still win for complex tooth movement. As a Diamond Invisalign provider, we will tell you plainly which is right for your case — and we may recommend veneers instead if your goal is purely cosmetic.

Care, cost & next steps

Aligner care, cost, and getting started

Aligners are medical devices that need daily care; looking after them keeps your treatment on schedule.

  • Remove them to eat, drink anything but water, brush, and floss. Leaving food or sugar trapped under a tray is the fastest route to decay.
  • Rinse and clean them daily. Use a soft brush and cool water — never hot water, which warps the plastic.
  • Keep them in the case when out. Aligners wrapped in a napkin are the most common way they get thrown away.
  • Wear them about 22 hours a day. Compliance, not the specific appliance, drives the result.[4]
