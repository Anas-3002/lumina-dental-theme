Sterilization

Hospital-grade sterilization you can ask to see

Sterilization destroys all microorganisms on an instrument, including the resistant bacterial spores that ordinary disinfection leaves behind.

Lumina classifies every item exactly as the CDC does. Critical items — anything that penetrates soft tissue or bone, such as surgical burs and extraction forceps — are always heat-sterilized after each use. Semicritical items that touch the gums are heat-sterilized too, and unwrapped “flash” sterilization is never used as a shortcut.[1] Each load is then verified mechanically, chemically and biologically, and our logs are available to any patient at the front desk.

“A spore test should be used at least weekly to monitor sterilizers… A spore test should also be used for every load with an implantable device.”

Centers for Disease Control and Prevention[2]

Implant accuracy

3D CBCT-guided implant planning

A CBCT (cone beam computed tomography) scan is a low-dose three-dimensional x-ray of the jaw that shows bone height, bone width and the exact path of the nerves in a single pass.

Flat x-rays hide how deep a nerve sits and how thin the bone is from cheek to tongue. A systematic review found CBCT gives “high accuracy and reliability for bony linear measurements… related to implant treatment,”[3] and a JADA review concluded virtual planning lets a clinician “create and visualize the end result before initiating treatment.”[4] We therefore choose the implant width and angle, and place it through a printed surgical guide, rather than judging by eye.
