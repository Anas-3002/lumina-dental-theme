Case studies

Three transformations, start to finish

These are anonymised clinical narratives drawn from our records; identifying details have been changed, and individual results vary.

Case one: a single front tooth, replaced

Before. A 41-year-old lost an upper central incisor after a cycling accident. She had avoided photographs for two years and only laughed with her hand up. A CBCT scan showed the bone was thinner than expected on the cheek side.

  • Day 0 — $99 Gateway: full exam, x-rays and a 3D CBCT scan.
  • Week 3 — guided implant placement using a printed surgical guide.
  • Month 4 — healing confirmed; digital scan taken for the crown.
  • Month 4–5 — custom ceramic crown fitted and shade-matched.

After. She was eating on that side within a week of the crown and reported no longer thinking about the tooth. Total: one implant, one crown, four appointments.

Case two: crowding straightened, colour evened

Before. A 29-year-old had mild to moderate lower crowding and had tried over-the-counter whitening, which left the colour uneven where teeth overlapped. This is the profile clear aligners suit best: mild to moderate crowding without extractions.[4]

  • Day 0 — intraoral scan; no impressions.
  • Week 3 — aligners issued; 10-day change interval.
  • Month 7 — active phase completed; whitening performed once teeth were aligned.
  • Month 7 onward — retainers at night.

After. The order matters: overlapping teeth whiten unevenly, which is exactly what had happened before she came to us. Align first, then whiten.

Case three: nine years of avoidance, then a plan

Before. A 52-year-old had not seen a dentist in nine years because of fear, and arrived in pain with bleeding gums and two cavities. He is not unusual: a meta-analysis of more than 72,000 adults estimated that 15.3% have dental fear and 12.4% a high level of it.[5]

  • Month 0 — $99 Gateway, exam only, Stop Paddle in hand; nothing else done that day.
  • Month 2 — hygiene and deep cleaning under sedation dentistry.
  • Month 3 — two fillings, one per visit.
  • Month 6 — routine maintenance; no sedation needed.

After. The first visit did nothing but look, which is the whole point. Fear is managed by giving control back, not by rushing to treatment. Read how on our sedation dentistry page.
